#  Sensitivity of Geographically Distributed Data-Centre Workload Allocation to Environmental Conditions
### Overview

This project investigates energy-efficient workload allocation in AI data centres, with a particular focus on the relationship between environmental conditions, cooling requirements, and computational energy consumption.

As AI workloads continue to grow, data centres face increasing energy demands, making efficient workload scheduling and cooling management important considerations for sustainable computing.

The project aims to develop a mathematical optimisation model to investigate how workload distribution and environmental conditions influence the overall energy consumption of data centre infrastructure.

**Project Objectives**

The main objectives of the project are:

Develop a mathematical optimisation model for workload allocation across data centre infrastructure.

Investigate the impact of environmental conditions, particularly ambient temperature, on cooling energy requirements.

Explore the relationship between computational workload distribution and overall facility energy consumption.

Evaluate the model under different environmental scenarios and investigate potential improvements through sensitivity analysis.

**Methodology**

The initial approach focuses on Linear Programming (LP) to formulate a mathematical model of workload allocation and energy consumption.

The investigation will consider factors such as:

Computational workload and associated energy requirements.

Data centre cooling systems and their operating conditions.

Environmental parameters affecting cooling efficiency.

Workload distribution across different data centre locations.

The model and its assumptions will be refined throughout the project as the literature review and experimental work progress.

**Technologies**

Python – Mathematical modelling, optimisation and simulation.

Linear Programming – Formulation and solution of the optimisation problem.

NumPy / Pandas – Numerical computation and data handling (where required).

Matplotlib – Visualisation and analysis of experimental results.
